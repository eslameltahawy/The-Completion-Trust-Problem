# D3 — Discovery and Validation Plan

No findings below are real research. Each item is a study that should happen before the MVP rule is treated as settled.

## 1. Employers can use the distinction

**Risk.** If partners do not look up IDs, or if they treat Not Verified as "this person failed," the portal does not create trust. It may penalize learners who only have a completion record.

**Who.** Employer-partner recruiters and hiring managers, plus the Career 180 partner manager.

**Method.** Fifteen moderated lookups on the partner page, using a verified ID, a revoked ID, a completion-record ID, and an unknown ID. Ask what decision they would make next, and whether absence of a certificate would count against a candidate.

**Evidence that would count.** They can state, unprompted, that a completion record is not a competency certificate, and they describe Not Verified as "no credential of this type," not as a failed skill.

**Design change.** If most partners treat a missing certificate as a negative signal, stop presenting assessment as an optional upgrade in employer-facing copy, and test a wording and process that blocks that inference before any wider release.

## 2. Review stays consistent and fast enough

**Risk.** Human review is the issuance gate. If reviewers disagree with each other, or if the queue ages past the point where learners and partners still care, the certificate is either slow or arbitrary.

**Who.** The people who would actually review, a Content & Instructors lead, and a legal or assessment owner. The instructor conversation is about payouts staying tied to completion, not about scoring learners.

**Method.** Give the same ten sample packages to three reviewers. Measure agreement, time, and the reasons they write. Do not use the demo evaluator as the definition of truth.

**Evidence that would count.** A documented agreement level the assessment owner accepts, and a median review time inside a limit they set before the exercise.

**Design change.** If agreement is poor, narrow the first competencies or change the rubric before adding courses. If time is unacceptable, do not "fix" it by auto-issuing on the current score. Revisit which assessments are in scope.

## 3. The assessment is acceptable evidence of the skill

**Risk.** A short quiz or practical task may not be what an employer needs, even when the workflow is clear. Shipping it would create a precise record of the wrong thing.

**Who.** Learners who finished the course, subject-matter authors, and two employer partners who hire for that skill.

**Method.** Talk through one quiz and one practical task. Ask what evidence would change a screening decision, and what would not. Compare that list with the current criteria. This is a product interview, not a validity study.

**Evidence that would count.** Partners name evidence the task actually collects, and learners can attempt it without hidden answer keys.

**Design change.** If partners want a work sample the task does not capture, keep the course at Assessment Design Required. Do not attach the current quiz and call it competency.
